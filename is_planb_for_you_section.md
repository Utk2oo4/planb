# CSEWhy PlanB --- "Is PlanB for You?" Section

## Purpose

Create a clear, balanced section that helps UPSC aspirants understand
whether CSEWhy PlanB fits their current goals. It should follow the
Career Opportunities section and maintain the same simple, professional
design language: warm-neutral background, black typography, signature
yellow highlights, subtle borders, and generous spacing.

The section should help visitors self-identify without shaming people
who do not need the program. Avoid fear-based messaging or implying that
choosing PlanB means giving up on UPSC.

## Brand design tokens

  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
  Token                   Value                   Usage
  ----------------------- ----------------------- -----------------------
  Signature yellow        `#FFD52F`               Headline highlights and
                                                  icon backgrounds

  Black                   `#111111`               Headings, text, icons

  White                   `#FFFFFF`               Main card surfaces

  Warm neutral            `#F7F5EF`               Section background

  Secondary text          `#555555`               Supporting copy

  Subtle border           `#E5E2DA`               Card outlines and
                                                  separators
  -----------------------------------------------------------------------

Use the yellow sparingly. Avoid dark green backgrounds, gradients,
glassmorphism, heavy shadows, and excessive animations.

## Section content

**Eyebrow:** `A PLAN THAT FITS YOUR JOURNEY`

**Headline:**

> Is UPSC PlanB for you?

Highlight `PlanB` with a rectangular `#FFD52F` background and black
text.

**Supporting copy:**

> PlanB is for aspirants who want to keep building skills and exploring
> career options while continuing their UPSC journey. It is not about
> giving up on your goal---it is about making room for more
> possibilities.

Keep the introduction centered and concise.

## Two comparison cards

Use two side-by-side cards on desktop, stacked on mobile. The cards
should have equal visual weight and clear headings. Avoid the reference
image's dark-green page background and brown panel; use the established
CSEWhy palette instead.

### Left card --- "PlanB may not be a priority for you if..."

**Icon:** A simple black outline circle with an X, on a subtle
warm-neutral or pale-yellow circle.

**Points:**

1.  You are fully focused on UPSC right now and are not looking to
    explore other career options.
2.  You already have a clear alternative career path and a plan to build
    the required skills.
3.  You have enough time and resources allocated to your current goals
    and do not need additional career guidance at this stage.
4.  You prefer to revisit career planning at a later point in your
    preparation.

Keep the wording respectful. Do not use phrases like "losers," "not
smart planners," or "risk your entire future."

### Right card --- "PlanB may be right for you if..."

**Icon:** A simple black outline checkmark, on a pale-yellow circle.

**Points:**

1.  You want to turn your UPSC knowledge into practical professional
    skills.
2.  You want to explore career options while continuing to prepare for
    the examination.
3.  You are interested in fields such as public policy, AI, research,
    consulting, communication, or social impact.
4.  You want to build experience, a portfolio, and confidence for future
    opportunities.

## Layout

### Desktop

-   Use a `#F7F5EF` section background.
-   Center the eyebrow, heading, and supporting copy.
-   Place two cards in a two-column grid beneath the introduction.
-   Keep both cards equal in width, with generous internal padding.
-   Use white card surfaces, thin `#E5E2DA` borders, and moderate corner
    radii.
-   Give the "may be right for you" card a subtle yellow top border or a
    small yellow heading accent.
-   Use simple icon-and-heading rows at the top of each card.
-   Present each point as a short, readable list item with consistent
    vertical spacing.

### Tablet

-   Keep two columns if the text remains comfortable to read.
-   Reduce card padding slightly and allow natural card height.

### Mobile

-   Stack the cards vertically.
-   Place the "may be right for you" card first if analytics or user
    testing suggests it should be prioritized; otherwise retain the
    desktop reading order.
-   Use left-aligned headings and list text.
-   Keep icons small and consistent.
-   Avoid horizontal scrolling.

## Closing statement

Below the two cards, add a centered closing line with a thin neutral
divider on either side, if it suits the layout.

**Suggested copy:**

> PlanB is not about replacing your UPSC goal. It is about keeping more
> doors open as you work toward it.

Highlight `keeping more doors open` with the signature yellow.

## Visual styling

-   Typeface: use the existing website font or a clean sans-serif such
    as Inter or Manrope.
-   Headline: bold black, with one yellow-highlighted phrase.
-   Card headings: bold black and clearly differentiated from list text.
-   Body text: `#555555` with comfortable line-height.
-   Icons: black outline icons in consistent size and stroke.
-   Card background: `#FFFFFF`.
-   Borders: `1px solid #E5E2DA`.
-   Radius: around `16px`.
-   Shadows: none or extremely subtle.
-   Hover: optional small border-color change; the cards do not need to
    animate if they are not interactive.

## Suggested sizing

-   Section padding: `72–96px` vertically on desktop and `48–64px` on
    mobile.
-   Content max-width: `1120–1200px`.
-   Headline: `clamp(2rem, 3.2vw, 3rem)`.
-   Supporting copy: maximum width around `800px`.
-   Card padding: `28–32px` desktop and `20px` mobile.
-   List spacing: `16–20px` between items.

Treat these as starting points and keep spacing consistent with the
hero, Why PlanB, and Career Opportunities sections.

## Optional CTA

If a relevant destination exists, add a small CTA below the closing
statement:

**Button:** `Explore Courses →`

Link it to the homepage courses section or the Courses page. Do not add
a button if the section already transitions directly into featured
courses.

## Accessibility and implementation

-   Use semantic section, heading, and list elements.
-   Do not rely on color alone to distinguish the two cards; use clear
    headings and X/check icons.
-   Ensure text contrast is readable.
-   Decorative icons should be hidden from assistive technology;
    meaningful icons should have accessible labels.
-   Preserve visible keyboard focus if any links or buttons are
    included.
-   Respect reduced-motion preferences.
-   Make the layout responsive and test at desktop, tablet, and mobile
    widths.

## Content and trust requirements

-   Do not imply that everyone needs PlanB.
-   Avoid fear-based messaging or claims that UPSC preparation is
    wasted.
-   Do not promise employment, income, or placements.
-   Make clear that PlanB can complement, rather than replace, UPSC
    preparation.
-   Keep the tone practical, supportive, and non-judgmental.
